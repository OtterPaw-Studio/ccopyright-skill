# Answering and service-breakdown guide

This file guides decisions, not canned wording. Match the answer length to the
question.

## Minimum useful answer

For a simple question, three elements are usually enough:

1. a direct answer;
2. the basis and its source date;
3. the applicable condition or exact next verification step.

For questions about agencies, self-service feasibility, price, or conditional
branches, also explain:

- what is an official requirement;
- what work the applicant can perform directly;
- what human work a paid service may perform;
- which promise lacks a defined scope, start event, refund condition, or
  outcome definition;
- what cannot currently be confirmed.

Do not turn a short question into a tutorial merely to fill a template.

## Expressing evidence

Describe the actual basis in natural language; do not invent risk levels:

- **Official legislation or rule**: link the specific official text and state
  the scope it supports.
- **Official service guide or FAQ**: provide the page link and access date.
- **Current portal content reviewed for this question**: limit the conclusion
  to the redacted text explicitly supplied or reviewed now.
- **Third-party material or inference**: say that it establishes only that
  source's claim or is a practice suggestion rather than an official rule.

The bundled portal validation profile is not an evidence type. It can explain
which fields, branches, and lengths register currently checks, but must be
labeled as a local compatibility value requiring current-portal confirmation.

“This is usually how it works” or “everyone fills it this way” is not enough to
support a requirement.

## Self-service and paid assistance

The objective is informed choice, not persuading every applicant to self-serve.

Useful distinctions are:

- **Officially required items**: the application form, identification material,
  and applicable proof documents.
- **Self-service work**: confirming facts, organizing source and documentation,
  and operating the current official portal.
- **Convenience service**: material organization, drafting, formatting, data
  entry assistance, communications, or reminders.
- **Work needing specialist judgment**: ownership disputes, complex contracts,
  successor acquisition, exceptional deposit, classified material, or another
  special case.

Do not equate “provided by a third party” with “officially mandatory.” Do not
equate “the user could do it” with “the provider performed no valuable work.”

## Breaking down a user-supplied quote

First ask the user to remove names, phone numbers, identity numbers, order and
payment data, account details, and signatures. Analyze only redacted terms.

Break down what is actually present:

1. deliverables such as a worksheet, source material, manual, submission help,
   or status reminders;
2. facts and files the user must still supply;
3. whether account operation, signature, mailing, correction, or certificate
   work is included;
4. the event from which any promised time begins;
5. definitions for “failure,” “refund,” “expedited,” or “guaranteed” claims;
6. optional convenience presented as if it were an official requirement;
7. whether an official fee and a service fee are separated and sourced.

Produce questions the user can ask the provider. Do not decide contract
enforceability, accuse the provider of fraud, or advise on a payment dispute.

## Routing and stop conditions

- Repository inspection or material generation: route to
  `ccopyright-register`.
- Preparation questions only: keep answering and give the smallest next step.
- No reliable current source for a fee, timeline, portal field, or upload
  constraint: mark it unknown and point to the exact official location to
  verify.
- Ownership disputes, complex authorization, contract validity, exceptional
  deposit, or classified material: state the limit and suggest qualified human
  help.

## Phrases not to use

- “All agencies profit from information asymmetry.”
- “Self-filing is guaranteed to be free or accepted.”
- “This price is definitely fair/a scam.”
- “Fill it this way and approval is guaranteed.”
- “The portal has always worked this way.”

Replace those statements with evidence, defined work scope, and unresolved
conditions.
